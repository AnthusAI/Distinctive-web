# Research: AI at the USPTO and the trademark landscape (October 2026)

First research pass, 2026-10-20, checked against primary sources. Seeds the knowledge base with labeled references and story ideas.

## Hypothesis tested

"AI is taking over trademark examination, driven by budget and staff cuts, possibly making examination more consistent but risking biased outcomes."

- **Supported:** AI is arriving in the work around examination. Class ACT (March 2026) assigns classes, design codes and pseudo marks, reviewed by humans; 250,000 applications by July. An AI image search (beta) and a mark-description generator arrived in April; Scout, the USPTO's general AI tool, reached every trademark unit in July. Examiner inconsistency is documented (Frakes and Wasserman, 69 Duke L.J. 1807, 2020: wide divergence across 7.8 million applications).
- **Overstated:** no tool decides registrability ("examining attorneys lead, tools support"). The push for consistency comes from the courts: *In re Brunetti* (Fed. Cir. Aug. 26, 2025) vacated a failure-to-function refusal for lack of a coherent standard. No reported bias, error or lawsuit about USPTO trademark AI; the gap is transparency (no published accuracy figures). Documented AI harm comes from filers (a 2025 reprimand for nonexistent citations in a TTAB brief; 2024 guidance on AI-generated specimens).
- **Not supported:** the USPTO is fee-funded; examining attorneys were exempt from the 2025 freeze and layoffs; it is hiring. Pendency is falling (first action 5.6 to 4.45 months in H1 FY2026, with filings up 7%).

## Landscape

- Supreme Court granted *RiseandShine v. PepsiCo* (No. 24-1016): is mark strength for the judge or the jury.
- Federal Circuit: *Curtin* (standing to oppose under *Lexmark*), *Brunetti* (failure to function), *Bayou Grande* (KAHWA, foreign equivalents).
- TTAB precedents are scarce, and recent ones lean cross-border (Brazilian, Mexican and offshore applicants).
- Fraud enforcement: 11 orders against about 10,500 records since October 2025, including filers impersonating the USPTO.
- Rules: Nice 13-2027 from Jan. 1, 2027; WIPO Madrid e-Filing designated for US-origin international applications; partial replacement under Madrid; 60-day TTAB answer period.
